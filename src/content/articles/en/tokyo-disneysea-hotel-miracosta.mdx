The easiest mistake to make at this hotel is reading a place name as a view. **The side tells you which part of the building you are in; only the view grade at the end of the name says anything about your window.** The cheapest room on the harbour side, Partial View, can face a wall and a gravel yard; a Venice Side room without “Palazzo Canals View” in its name can look at the roof of the spa.

The hotel has 502 rooms. The official list files several bed layouts and occupancies under one heading — the same “Superior Room (Piazza View)” covers a double, a twin and a 40 m² four-guest plan — so this site splits them wherever the booking decision changes, giving {ROOMS.length} types.

## The building is part of the harbour

The Italian harbour-town facades you look up at from inside the park have guest rooms and corridors behind them, and a Hotel & Park Gateway on the second floor connects straight into it. The cost of that is a window priced as a product in its own right: across comparable 37 m² Superior Rooms, Partial View to Harbor View changes almost nothing indoors and ¥28,000 on the rate.

## The three sides are not three views

### Toscana Side: the entrance and the AquaSphere

Toscana Side faces the hotel's main entrance and the Tokyo DisneySea entrance. All six combinations carry the Capitano Mickey seafaring theme, with 37 m² Superior Rooms from about ¥83,000 and a 43 m² Triple Room from about ¥88,500 — the lowest threshold in the hotel. An ordinary Toscana room comes with no view guarantee; about ¥5,500 more buys “DisneySea AquaSphere View”, which promises the AquaSphere at the park entrance rather than the harbour inside it.

### Venezia Side: canals, a spa, and two kinds of outdoor space

Venezia Side runs along the Palazzo Canals and Terme Venezia. An ordinary Superior Room starts at about ¥83,000, the same as Toscana; without “Palazzo Canals View” in the name the gondola waterway is not promised, and about ¥5,500 turns it into a guarantee. Two options exist nowhere else in the hotel: the ground-floor **Palazzo Patio Room** with a private patio, and the second-floor **Balcony Room**, added in 2025, which lets you step outside on the canal side without lounge access or breakfast and sleeps four.

### Porto Paradiso Side: the longest names, and where the view premium really happens

Porto Paradiso Side faces the harbour town on Mediterranean Harbor, but inside it there are four further grades: Partial, Piazza, Piazza Grand and Harbor. **Stopping at the words “Porto Paradiso Side” is the most expensive misreading in the whole booking process.**

“Speciale Rooms & Suites” is not a fourth side but a service grade spanning the Venice and Porto sides: the fifth-floor Salone dell'Amico, next-morning breakfast, and a dedicated check-in counter.

- **Partial View**, from about ¥83,000: faces the harbour town with a wall or a roof in the way. A guest in room 3147 called what was straight ahead a “gravel view”.
- **Piazza View**, from about ¥93,000: Piazza Topolino and the harbour-town streets. Some positions catch a slice of water, but that is allocation, not the name.
- **Piazza Grand View**, from about ¥104,900: adds part of the harbour to the piazza and the streets.
- **Harbor View**, from about ¥111,000: officially a full view of the Porto Paradiso harbour. The floor for watching a water show from indoors.
- **Harbor Grand View**: rooms 5353 and 5357 only, and the official definition includes viewing it *from the spacious terrace* of about 30 m².

## What watching a water show from your room really buys

The angle depends on floor and room number: some positions lean towards the American Waterfront, others towards Zambini Brothers. Fixed glass, reflections and the room's own lighting all show up in photographs, and an ordinary window does not open. What only a guest sees is everything outside the scheduled show: boats being tested, performers moving into position, the empty harbour before opening, the street going dark one lamp at a time after it closes.

## 37 types, built from a handful of parts

- **Superior Room**: the base plan, usually 37 m² for three guests; the four-guest version is 40 m² with a cruise bed added.
- **Triple Room**: 43 m² with three 120 cm regular beds, so three adults means nobody on a low trundle bed.
- **Palazzo Patio Room**: Venezia Side ground floor, 37 m², private patio, sleeps four.
- **Balcony Room**: floors 2–3, with outdoor access. The Venice version is not Speciale; the Porto version is.
- **Terrace Room**: all on the fifth floor, 43 m² inside plus about 30 m² of terrace, one 160 cm double, sleeps two.
- **Harbor Room**: 60 m², two large windows, sleeps four. This is a layout name — read the bracket after it for the view.
- **Suites**: Porto Paradiso Suite 81 m², MiraCosta Suite 87 m², Il Magnifico Suite 199 m². “Porto Paradiso” in the name does not mean the window holds the whole harbour.

Official layout drawings, areas, occupancies, beds, floors and month-by-month rates for all {ROOMS.length} types are in the <a href={localePath('en', '/hotels/tokyo-disneysea-hotel-miracosta')}>room database</a>.

### The fourth bed is not a normal bed

A four-guest room is usually two regular beds, a trundle bed and a cruise bed, and the cruise bed is **84 × 182 cm**. “Sleeps four” means the hotel will sell it to four people, not that there are four equal beds. For three adults, a 43 m² Triple Room costs only about ¥5,500 more than a 37 m² Superior.

Children aged 11 and under can share a bed: one per regular bed, two per 180 cm double. A Terrace Room's 160 cm double also takes two at most.

## What the room numbers tell you, and what they do not

The Japanese hotel-research site dhoteloo has gone through the Porto Paradiso side room by room. **178 rooms** can be checked against it:

- The first digit is the floor, the last three the position, so 3125, 4125 and 5125 are the same spot on different floors.
- Every number listed is odd; the even numbers are not in the dataset.
- The 1xx run exists only on floors 3–5; the 3xx run is the only one with second-floor rooms, the first floor having no Porto Paradiso rooms and the second only reaching partway along.
- A position can change grade on the fifth floor: 3127 and 4127 are ordinary Harbor View rooms, 5127 the Speciale version with lounge access and breakfast.
- The Partial View numbers are on the plans but in none of the text lists: 3147 through 3153 and 4147 through 4153 at the far end of the north-west wing, and 2327, 3327, 4327 and 5327 at the bend in the corridor.

The same source carries hand-drawn plans of floors 2 to 5 with a room number in every cell. This site has traced them to scale over aerial photography and coloured each cell by its official view grade: <a href={localePath('en', '/hotels/tokyo-disneysea-hotel-miracosta#floor-plan')}>the plan is in the room database</a>, and typing a room number jumps to its cell. The Tuscany and Venice sides get position and orientation without a room type.

The hotel states plainly that it accepts no request for a specific location or floor. You can express a preference, but nothing is guaranteed.
</Callout>

### Connecting rooms have to be decided before you book

The same survey lists seven pairs of categories that can be connected on the Porto side: two Piazza View Superior Rooms, a Harbor View Superior Room with a Harbor Room (Harbor View), even the accessible Superior Room with a Porto Paradiso Suite — the last visible in the numbers, room 3103 sitting between suites 3101 and 3105. Connecting is not an option in the booking form: **both room types have to be reserved together, then the hotel telephoned with the request, and even then it is not guaranteed.**

## Balcony versus Terrace is not a translation quirk

|  | Balcony Room | Terrace Room |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Floor | 2–3 | 5 |
| Indoor area | 37–43 m² | 43 m² |
| Sleeps | Up to 4 | 2 |
| Outdoor space | A small balcony along the facade | About 30 m² of terrace |
| Beds | Twin plus extra beds | One 160 cm double |
| Speciale | Not on the Venice side; yes on the Porto side | Always |

Both depend on **the weather.** One guest booked the new Venice Balcony Room and met a typhoon, retreating indoors in light rain without taking a single photograph; another, staying in January, found the sun strong enough to sit outside with a coffee in a coat.

## The Speciale premium: put breakfast back in first

Speciale Rooms & Suites guests can use the Salone dell'Amico on the fifth floor from 14:00 on the day of arrival until 12:00 on the day of departure, check in and out at a dedicated counter, help themselves to drinks, and have next-morning breakfast included. A Harbor View Superior Room either side of that line: ordinary 37 m² for three guests from about ¥111,000, Speciale 40 m² for three from about ¥130,000 — ¥19,000 apart.

Only **suites, Harbor Rooms and Terrace Rooms** may take breakfast in the room instead of the BellaVista Lounge buffet. Within the grade, a Harbor View Harbor Room starts around ¥152,000, below the Harbor View Balcony Room's ¥161,500: 20 m² more and two large windows instead of a door to the outside.

## Getting around: a door in the hotel is not a morning route

From beside Maihama Station, the Disney Resort Line runs to Tokyo DisneySea Station; leave through the gate at the front of the train and a walkway leads straight to the hotel's second floor. The monorail is a paid service and is not part of the room rate. The hotel's own gateway to the park:

- **Leaving Tokyo DisneySea for the hotel: available all day.**
- **Entering or re-entering the park from the hotel: only from one hour after normal opening.**
- **Happy Entry in the morning: not through this door. Use the designated north security check.**

### Happy Entry is no longer a flat 15 minutes

MiraCosta guests get Happy Entry at both parks: 15 minutes at Tokyo Disneyland, and **5 to 15 minutes** at Tokyo DisneySea depending on the day, because of construction around the entrance. It does not apply on the first night, only on following days of the stay and on departure day. The pass handed over at check-in carries that day's start time, and you show it with a valid park ticket after clearing security.

<Callout locale="en" type="tip" title="Send the luggage ahead on arrival day">
Between 7:30 and 16:00 you can hand luggage in on the second floor of the Tokyo Disney Resort Welcome Center in front of JR Maihama Station and have it delivered to the hotel free. Sending it back on departure day is a paid service: the hotel accepts bags from 7:00 to 12:30, collection from 13:00 to 21:00.
</Callout>

## Restaurants and the pool

- **BellaVista Lounge**: large windows facing Mediterranean Harbor, serving breakfast, lunch and dinner, with harbour shows visible from some seats at some times.
- **Oceano**: Mediterranean food, buffet and course menus in parallel, the largest seating in the hotel. The official page says a show can be seen from the terrace at some times, though whether it opens depends on operations that day.
- **Silk Road Garden**: Cantonese food, and the only one of the three with no breakfast service.

All three are popular, and staying in the hotel does not guarantee a table; breakfast also competes with Happy Entry for the same minutes.

Terme Venezia is a spa and pool for hotel guests only, but **not a free benefit** — admission is bought by the day. The indoor main pool, warm pool, children's pool, jacuzzi and sauna run year-round, the outdoor pool is summer only, and the whole thing closes for renovation from 4 November 2026 to 19 February 2027.

## Booking in practice: window, then beds, then service

Room-only reservations usually open **four months before the stay, on the same date, at 11:00 Japan time**, for up to five nights and three rooms at once; when that month has no matching date, bookings open on the first of the following month.

Settle three things first — the window (don't care, streets, part of the harbour, all of it), the beds (a double, three regular beds, or four people accepting a cruise bed), and whether the lounge and breakfast earn back the Speciale difference — then put the answers into the <a href={localePath('en', '/hotels/tokyo-disneysea-hotel-miracosta#room-explorer')}>room finder</a> and search the full official room name when booking opens.

For a type with two rooms, like 5353 and 5357, the constraint is supply rather than budget; the ordinary Harbor View twin has 36 rooms. Released inventory has no fixed hour.

## Construction and closures to avoid, 2026–2028

- **19 Aug – 1 Dec 2026**: lift replacement at the front end of Tokyo DisneySea Station. The walkway stays usable by stairs; wheelchairs, strollers and large luggage need the official alternative route.
- **4 Nov 2026 – 19 Feb 2027**: Terme Venezia renovation closes the spa and both pools.
- **From 13 Jan 2027, in stages**: the Venice Terrace Room, the Porto Terrace Rooms, the Speciale Superior Rooms, the Balcony Rooms and the Il Magnifico Suite come out of sale for room refurbishment, on different dates for each type.
- **13 Apr 2027 – 10 Apr 2028**: both Toscana AquaSphere View types come out of sale for construction around the Tokyo DisneySea entrance.
- **Entrance work runs into fiscal 2028**: the Happy Entry security check and early-entry window may keep changing.

Every published closure is on the relevant room card in the database. If your stay falls in 2027, open the official room page once more before paying.

## Four lines to finish on

- **First visit, watching the budget**: Toscana with AquaSphere View or Venezia with Palazzo Canals View — about ¥5,500 over the no-view version turns a lottery into a guarantee.
- **You want the water show from the room**: search for Harbor View directly, and do not be carried off by a side name, by Piazza Grand, or by the word Harbor in Harbor Room.
- **Three adults**: start with the Triple Room, for six square metres more and a proper 120 cm bed each.
- **You want outdoor space**: decide between the immediacy of a low balcony and the size of a fifth-floor terrace, then price in the weather.

The hotel sells four separate things: a location inside the park, a window, beds, and service. Pay for them separately and you will not end up in the room with the longest name and the highest rate that happens to suit you least.
